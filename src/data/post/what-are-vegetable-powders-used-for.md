## What is vegetable powder?

Vegetable powder is made by dehydrating and drying fresh vegetables (such as freeze-drying or spray-drying). Because it retains most of the vegetables’ nutrients, is rich in dietary fiber and vitamins, and is easy to store, highly concentrated, and convenient to use, its applications have expanded from simple food processing to multiple fields such as health management.

As a major vegetable producer, China has seen steady growth in exports of dehydrated vegetables and vegetable powders. The vegetable powder market is shifting from “traditional dehydrated vegetables” to “functional superfoods.”

Driven by the trends of “Clean Label” and “plant-based,” demand is primarily concentrated in food processing. In addition, the products are trending towards multifunctionality, no longer being single vegetable powders, but mixed vegetable powders designed for “sleep, anti-oxidation, and gut health” (such as: kale + turmeric + probiotics).

## Nutritional supplementation and health management

Dietary fiber supplementation. Vegetable powders like kale powder and barley grass powder are rich in fiber, which helps promote bowel movements and improve constipation.

Meal replacements and weight management. Adding vegetable powder to meal replacement formulas can increase satiety and supplement nutrients while controlling calorie intake.

Infant and toddler food supplements. Vegetable powder particles are finely ground and easily digested and absorbed, making them suitable for mixing into infant and toddler foods. Vegetable powder is a convenient and highly nutritious source of vegetable alternatives.

Outdoor and emergency rescue: ideal for hiking, sailing, or military operations.

## Industrial food processing

Vegetable powder serves both flavoring and coloring purposes. In the food industry, vegetable powder is widely used due to its good stability and ease of formulation.

Natural food coloring, in place of artificial colors. Examples include beetroot powder (red/pink), spinach powder (green), pumpkin powder (yellow), and purple sweet potato powder (purple).

Flavor and thickening. Tomato powder, shiitake mushroom powder, and onion powder are commonly used as natural seasonings to enhance the umami flavor of dishes. Pumpkin powder or potato powder can be used for thickening sauces or making soups, adding texture.

Baking ingredients. Mixing vegetable powders into bread or cookies improves nutritional value and gives the product a distinct flavor.

Snacks. Used as a flavoring on puffed foods, such as potato chips, in tomato, cucumber, or spicy profiles.

Improving the quality of meat products. Some vegetable powders, such as celery powder, contain natural nitrates and are used as natural preservatives and color-protecting agents in processed meat products such as bacon and sausages.
